## 3. Findings

Severity weighs how likely the problem is at today's volume against what goes wrong. Evidence is "Ran" where this pass saw the behaviour, and "Read" where it comes from reading the code.

### Accepted risk

| ID | Severity | Status | Problem | Reference | Owner |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| G1 | High | **Accepted risk** until the demo accounts are deleted after the hackathon. Not counted against the result | A demo admin can gain lasting admin access through account-management routes other than the direct create and promote paths. Its row in `docs/agent-runs/security-verifier.md` still reads "Open, high"; `SECURITY.md` records it as accepted | G1 in `docs/agent-runs/security-verifier.md`; T27 covers the direct paths only | `dev-agent`, to delete the accounts after the hackathon |

Closing this risk takes more than deleting the demo accounts. By reading `staff.service.ts`, turning demo off another account hands back a fresh password, and a demo admin can set the password of a non-demo admin. Access gained that way would outlast the demo accounts. Before closing G1, check the audit log for `staff.demo` entries and `passwordReset` updates made by demo accounts, and reset any affected passwords.
